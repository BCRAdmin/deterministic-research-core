## MSFT review

### Verdict

**MSFT is a good internal draft and a usable Mega-Cap / Cloud / AI Infrastructure template candidate.**

The report includes the right current-period KPI anchors:

- Q3 revenue: $82.89B
- Microsoft Cloud revenue: $54.50B
- Microsoft Cloud growth: 29.0%
- Azure growth: 40.0%
- AI annual revenue run-rate above $37.00B
- TTM revenue: $311.90B
- FCF: $67.65B
- SBC/Revenue: 3.9%
- EV/Sales: 9.59x
- P/FCF: 45.28x
- RSI: 52.59
- 50-SMA: 396.98
- 200-SMA: 467.02

### What works

The `Hold` rating is plausible. The report correctly frames Microsoft as a high-quality cloud and AI platform where business quality is strong, but the market still needs proof that AI capacity spending translates into durable free-cash-flow leverage.

The current-period KPI section is clear and useful. The Azure and AI run-rate claims are strong enough to make the report company-specific, not generic.

### What still needs improvement

The final rating section has one awkward sentence:

> Microsoft has a credible business case, but cloud and AI demand are strong, but valuation and AI capex conversion still require discipline.

This should be rewritten as:

> Microsoft has a credible AI and cloud growth case, but the stock still requires discipline because valuation already reflects much of that strength and investors need evidence that AI capex is converting into durable FCF leverage.

The report should also discuss capex/FCF conversion more concretely. It mentions capex intensity, but does not quantify capex or explain how it interacts with FCF.

### MSFT assessment

- Internal usability: good
- External publishability: close, but needs a writing pass
- Rating plausibility: good
- Template value: high for Mega-Cap Cloud / AI Infrastructure

## GOOGL review

### Verdict

**GOOGL is the strongest report in the bundle and remains a gold-standard-v1 template for Mega-Cap / Ads / Cloud.**

The report includes the required current-period KPI anchors:

- Q1 revenue: $109.90B
- Google Cloud revenue: $20.00B
- Cloud growth: 63.0%
- Q1 operating margin: 36.1%
- Q1 capex: $35.67B
- TTM FCF: $64.43B
- Other Income gain: $37.70B
- RSI: 81.33
- 50-SMA: 318.42
- 200-SMA: 283.15

### What works

The report now reads like an analyst draft rather than a claim ledger. The central investment debate is clear:

- Alphabet has excellent operating assets.
- Google Cloud is growing rapidly.
- AI infrastructure capex is heavy.
- FCF remains strong but conversion quality matters.
- The stock is technically stretched.

The `Hold` rating is well justified. It avoids both a naive Buy and an overly bearish Sell.

### What still needs improvement

The valuation section is directionally right but still shallow. EV/Sales and P/FCF are useful, but a stronger report would add:

- how much of the valuation is supported by Search cash generation,
- whether Google Cloud growth can justify multiple support,
- what happens if AI capex stays elevated for longer,
- what FCF conversion level would justify upgrading.

The Evidence Appendix is still too dense for external use, but acceptable for internal research.

### GOOGL assessment

- Internal usability: strong
- External publishability: near-publishable with editing
- Rating plausibility: strong
- Template value: strong gold-standard-v1

## SNOW review

### Verdict

**SNOW is a strong SaaS / Consumption / Data Platform internal template.**

The report includes the right Snowflake-specific KPI anchors:

- Product revenue: $4.47B
- NRR: 125.0%
- RPO: $9.77B
- Customers above $1M product revenue: 733
- Adjusted FCF: $1.19B
- SBC/Revenue: 26.4%
- TTM revenue: $4.34B
- FCF: $1.12B
- EV/Sales: 10.79x
- P/FCF: 42.70x
- price below 50-SMA and 200-SMA
- RSI: 44.76

### What works

The report’s `Tactical Underweight` rating is plausible. It distinguishes between:

- business quality: still relevant and cash generative,
- equity setup: not yet attractive because the chart is damaged and SBC remains high.

This is exactly the kind of nuanced rating language the system should produce.

The report correctly avoids a full Sell, because Product Revenue, NRR, RPO and adjusted FCF show that Snowflake is still strategically relevant.

### What still needs improvement

The report should be more explicit about the consumption-model risk. It mentions consumption volatility and RPO conversion, but a stronger version would explain:

- why NRR of 125% is good but no longer enough by itself,
- how RPO conversion interacts with product revenue growth,
- why SBC/Revenue at 26.4% weakens equity-holder quality,
- what technical repair would be enough to move from Tactical Underweight to Hold.

### SNOW assessment

- Internal usability: strong
- External publishability: close with editing
- Rating plausibility: strong
- Template value: strong gold-standard-v1

## Cross-report assessment

### What is now working

1. **Publish report exists and is readable.**
   The new `publish_report.md` format is much better than the prior claim-ledger format.

2. **Current KPIs are in the main body.**
   This was the biggest prior blocker and is now largely fixed.

3. **Internal pipeline language is gone from the main body.**
   The reports no longer read like DecisionPacket output.

4. **Ratings are plausible.**
   MSFT and GOOGL as Hold, SNOW as Tactical Underweight all make sense.

5. **Evidence remains available.**
   The appendix keeps traceability without polluting the main narrative.

### What still needs refinement

1. **Valuation is still shallow.**
   The reports cite EV/Sales and P/FCF, but they do not yet do enough implied-expectations or sensitivity work.

2. **Scenario analysis is too light.**
   Bull and Bear sections are directionally correct but not yet model-like.

3. **Action plans need sharper triggers.**
   They should include clearer upgrade/downgrade triggers, e.g. technical levels, FCF conversion thresholds, KPI trends.

4. **Appendix is too dense for external users.**
   Fine for internal research, too heavy for external publication.

5. **MSFT needs one sentence-level writing fix.**
   The final rating central debate sentence has awkward repetition.
